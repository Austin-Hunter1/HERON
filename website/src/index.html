Mapping surface water with reflected satellite signals

GPS, Galileo and BeiDou satellites transmit all the time. Some of that signal bounces off the ground, and water reflects it much more strongly than dry soil. HERON flies a drone that records the direct and the reflected signals, then measures where the water is.

In one paragraph

A drone carries two kinds of antenna: one looks up at the satellites, and one looks down at the ground. Four software-defined radios, locked to one shared clock, record raw radio samples from both on two GNSS frequency bands. After the flight, ground software finds each satellite in the recordings, compares the reflected signal with the direct one, and uses precise drone positions to place each reflection on a map.

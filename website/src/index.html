GNSS from a UAV

Two paths from one satellite

Each satellite reaches the drone twice: directly, and by reflection from the surface below. The reflected path is longer by about \(2h\sin e\), where \(h\) is the height above the surface and \(e\) the satellite elevation. Each measurement senses an ellipse on the ground, the first Fresnel zone. From a drone it is metres across, not the kilometres of a satellite.

The science

From radio samples to water

The payload records the direct and the reflected signal on one clock. The ground software tracks the direct signal, predicts the reflection from the geometry (open-loop tracking), and measures three things in each reflection: its power, its coherence, and its carrier phase. From these come four products.

Geometry and footprint

$$\Delta\rho=\rho_R-\rho_D\approx 2h\sin e$$ $$b=\sqrt{\frac{\lambda h}{\sin e}},\qquad a=\frac{b}{\sin e}$$

Excess path of the reflection, and the semi-axes of the first Fresnel zone (\(\lambda\) = 25.5 cm at L5). Camps & Munoz-Martin (2020); Martín-Neira (1993).

Open-loop reflected phase

$$\phi_R(t)=\phi_D(t)-\frac{D_p(t)}{\lambda}+\phi_{hw}$$

The reflected carrier phase is predicted from the direct channel and the modelled excess path \(D_p\). Both channels share one sample clock, so the receiver clock, the satellite clock and the ionosphere cancel. Zavorotny & Voronovich (2000); Wang & Morton (2022).

Water detection

$$\kappa=\frac{\left|\sum_{q} Z_q\right|^2}{W\sum_{q}\left|Z_q\right|^2},\qquad R=\left|\frac{1}{W}\sum_{q} e^{\,j w_q}\right|$$

Calm water gives a coherent reflection: \(\kappa\) and the circular length \(R\) of the phase stay near 1; rough land gives values near 0. The reflected power rises with the water fraction of the Fresnel zone. Roesler et al. (2022); Loria et al. (2023).

Altimetry

$$h_\varphi(t)=h_{\mathrm{RTK}}(t)+\frac{r(t)}{2\sin e(t)}$$

The tracked phase residual \(r\) (a Kalman filter on the reflected phase) corrects the modelled path, giving the receiver height above the surface to centimetres. With the RTK position this is the water-surface elevation. Zhang & Morton (2023); Xu et al. (2025).

Bank slope

$$\Delta s=-\frac{r}{2\sin e},\qquad \tan\beta=\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\mathrm{d}x}$$

Over the shore, the same phase gives the ground height \(s\) above the water along the specular track; its gradient across the shoreline is the bank slope \(\beta\). HERON checks it against airborne lidar.

River slope

$$S=\frac{\hat H_{\mathrm{up}}-\hat H_{\mathrm{down}}}{D_{\mathrm{centerline}}}$$

Water-surface elevations upstream and downstream, separated by the distance along the river centreline. Wang & Morton (2022); Wang et al. (2025).

The FISH

The payload: four radios, one clock

The FISH payload hangs under an Aurelia X6 hexacopter. A zenith RHCP antenna receives the direct signals; a nadir antenna receives the reflections in RHCP and LHCP. Two Ettus B200 and two B210 software-defined radios record six channels on two bands (L1 and L5). A HERON-designed clock board gives all four the same 10 MHz reference and pulse-per-second, so every channel is coherent. An Intel NUC writes the raw samples to an SSD at 264 MB/s.

Mission planning

Fly where the reflections land

The HERON mission planner places the drone so that the reflections of chosen satellites fall on a lake, a river or a mapped area at the planned time. It then flies and monitors the mission.

  • Plan: route, area sweep, shoreline following, or a science sequence; reflection tracks and Fresnel-zone coverage on a satellite map; GPS, Galileo and BeiDou.
  • Export: ArduPilot waypoints with heading and recording segments.
  • Simulate: a demo aircraft, or ArduCopter SITL, with the payload in the loop.
  • Fly: launch checks, live instruments, payload and RTK base status, return home.

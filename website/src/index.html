GNSS from a UAV

Two paths from one satellite

Each satellite reaches the drone twice: directly, and by reflection from the surface below. The reflected path is longer by about \(2h\sin e\), where \(h\) is the height above the surface and \(e\) the satellite elevation. Each measurement senses an ellipse on the ground, the first Fresnel zone. From a drone it is about 10 × 20 m; from a satellite it is about a kilometre.

The science

What water science needs, and how we measure it

Rivers, lakes, reservoirs and wetlands drive floods, droughts and water supply. Hydrologists need to know where the water is, how high it stands, and how steeply it falls, at the scale of a stream or a shoreline and as often as conditions change. Gauges are precise but sparse; optical satellites stop at clouds; radar altimeters see only along their ground tracks (Xie et al., 2025). A reflected GNSS signal measured from a drone can fill that gap. Open each need to see the math.

01Where is the water?Water extent and shorelines

Calm water reflects GNSS signals coherently, like a mirror; land and vegetation scatter them. The reflected power and the steadiness of its phase therefore mark open water, for each first Fresnel zone along the track (Loria et al., 2023).

$$\kappa=\frac{\left|\sum_{q} Z_q\right|^2}{W\sum_{q}\left|Z_q\right|^2},\qquad R=\left|\frac{1}{W}\sum_{q} e^{\,j w_q}\right|$$

\(Z_q\) are the reflected correlator sums over \(W\) consecutive 20 ms periods and \(w_q\) their phases. Over water \(\kappa\) and the circular length \(R\) stay near 1; for noise alone \(\mathrm{E}[\kappa]\approx 1/W\) (Roesler et al., 2022). The reflected power responds to the water fraction of the footprint,

$$f=\frac{|E\cap W_{\mathrm{water}}|}{|E|},$$

with \(E\) the Fresnel-zone ellipse; a shoreline is where \(f\) crosses 0.5.

02How high is the water?Water-surface elevation (altimetry)

The reflected path is longer than the direct one by an amount set by the height of the receiver above the surface (Martín-Neira, 1993):

$$\Delta\rho=\rho_R-\rho_D\approx 2h\sin e .$$

HERON predicts this path from the RTK trajectory and the satellite orbit, and tracks the reflected carrier phase against the prediction. Both channels share one clock, so the receiver clock, the satellite clock and the ionosphere cancel:

$$\phi_R(t)=\phi_D(t)-\frac{D_p(t)}{\lambda}+\phi_{hw}.$$

A Kalman filter on the phase residual gives the correction \(r\) (in metres) to the modelled path, and from it the height above the water to centimetres:

$$h_\varphi(t)=h_{\mathrm{RTK}}(t)+\frac{r(t)}{2\sin e(t)},\qquad \mathrm{WSE}=z_{\mathrm{RTK}}-h_\varphi .$$

At L5, one cycle of phase (\(\lambda\) = 25.5 cm) is a few centimetres of height (Zhang & Morton, 2023; Xu et al., 2025).

03How steep is the bank?Shore and bank slope

Where the reflection moves onto the shore, the same phase measures the ground instead of the water. Relative to the water surface, the ground height along the specular track is

$$\Delta s=-\frac{r}{2\sin e},$$

and the bank slope \(\beta\) is its gradient along the track, across the shoreline:

$$\tan\beta=\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\mathrm{d}x}.$$

The footprint sets the resolution: at 150 ft and 30° elevation the first Fresnel zone is about 10 × 19 m (Camps & Munoz-Martin, 2020). HERON checks these heights against airborne lidar.

04How fast does the river fall?River slope

River slope controls discharge and is hard to measure at scale. From water-surface elevations upstream and downstream, separated by the distance along the river centreline:

$$S=\frac{\hat H_{\mathrm{up}}-\hat H_{\mathrm{down}}}{D_{\mathrm{centerline}}}.$$

From orbit, carrier-phase GNSS-R has measured slopes of a few centimetres per kilometre (Wang & Morton, 2022; Wang et al., 2025). From a drone, the footprint is small enough for streams.

The FISH

The payload: four radios, one clock

The FISH payload hangs under an Aurelia X6 hexacopter. A zenith RHCP antenna receives the direct signals; a nadir antenna receives the reflections in RHCP and LHCP. Two Ettus B200 and two B210 software-defined radios record six channels on two bands (L1 and L5). A HERON-designed clock board gives all four the same 10 MHz reference and pulse-per-second, so every channel is coherent. An Intel NUC writes the raw samples to an SSD at 264 MB/s.

Mission planning

Fly where the reflections land

The HERON mission planner places the drone so that the reflections of chosen satellites fall on a lake, a river or a mapped area at the planned time. It then flies and monitors the mission.

  • Plan: route, area sweep, shoreline following, or a science sequence; reflection tracks and Fresnel-zone coverage on a satellite map; GPS, Galileo and BeiDou.
  • Export: ArduPilot waypoints with heading and recording segments.
  • Simulate: a demo aircraft, or ArduCopter SITL, with the payload in the loop.
  • Fly: launch checks, live instruments, payload and RTK base status, return home.

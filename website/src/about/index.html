Concept of operations

Plan, fly, record, verify

  1. Plan. The mission planner places the specular points on the target water and writes ArduPilot waypoints.
  2. Set up. The GNSS base station starts logging and sends RTK corrections; the payload boots and its radios lock to the clock board.
  3. Launch. The ground station starts the recording over the autopilot's telemetry link. If no command arrives, the payload starts recording on its own after 120 s.
  4. Survey and record. The drone flies the specular track; four SDRs record six channels of raw samples. If the link drops, the payload keeps recording.
  5. Land and offload. The team stops the recording and copies the SSD and the rover and base GNSS logs.
  6. Process and verify. The processing software tracks the signals, computes the precise trajectory, rebuilds the specular tracks and compares them with the plan.
Heritage · 2022–2023

SURGE: where HERON began

SURGE (Surface-water UAV Reflectometry GNSS Experiment) was the CU Boulder graduate project that put a GNSS-R receiver on the Aurelia X6 before HERON. Its goal was a proof-of-concept UAV system to determine the surface slopes, heights and extents of lakes and rivers, inspired by spaceborne river-slope measurements such as Wang and Morton's (2022) work on the Orinoco River.

The SURGE team designed and built the first payload: a single B210 software-defined radio recording direct and reflected GPS L5 signals to an Intel NUC, a PPK receiver (ArduSimple simpleRTK2B) for 10 cm positions, a gimbal camera, and a ground station in Mission Planner. They wrote the flight checklists, the crew roles and the first acquisition and specular-point scripts, and obtained an FAA waiver for flights above 400 ft.

HERON inherits SURGE's airframe, its operating procedures and its lessons: model RF behaviour, but test early; design for flights over water; and plan for the time that signal-processing software takes.

The SURGE team

Cody Watson, Daniel Mathews, Andreas Brecl, Jamie Stankiewiz, Sean Newman, Aaron Buller and Nicolas Garzione.

What SURGE set out to do

  • Record direct and reflected GPS L5 at 22 MHz or more, and L1 at 10 MHz or more.
  • Store correlated time and position data for PPK at 1 Hz, to 10 cm.
  • Fly autonomously along a flight path based on GPS ephemeris and the water body's shape, within 5 m.
  • Report payload and aircraft status to a ground station.
  • Record video of the water body for validation.
  • Process the data for relative surface height, water slope and water-body boundaries.

Source: Watson, C., Mathews, D., Brecl, A., Stankiewiz, J., Newman, S., Buller, A., & Garzione, N. (2023). SURGE: Surface-water Reflectometry GNSS Experiment [Final report]. University of Colorado Boulder. Read the SURGE final report (PDF) · SURGE code on GitHub

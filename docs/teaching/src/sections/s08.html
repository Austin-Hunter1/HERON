GNSS signals in HERON: seven signals, two bands

{{MIN}} MIN · BPSK, BOC, MBOC · SIGNAL PLAN

HERON records two bands, and three constellations share each band. They all use the same idea (a ±1 code on a carrier), but the newer signals shape each chip with a square-wave subcarrier. That moves power away from the band centre and sharpens the correlation peak.

How the information goes onto the carrier

BPSK(n): each chip is a flat ±1 pulse at \(n\times1.023\) MHz. The spectrum is a sinc² with its main lobe \(\pm n\times1.023\) MHz wide.

$$G_{BPSK}(f)=T_c\,\operatorname{sinc}^2(\pi f T_c)$$

BOC(m,n): multiply each chip by a square wave at \(m\times1.023\) MHz. The power splits into two lobes at \(\pm m\times1.023\) MHz. The correlation peak gets narrower, so code tracking is more precise, but side peaks appear. A receiver must not lock on a side peak.

$$s(t)=\sum_k c_k\,p(t-kT_c),\qquad p(t)=\operatorname{sign}\!\big(\sin 2\pi f_s t\big),\ \ 0\le t\lt T_c$$ $$G(f)=\frac{1}{T_c}\,\big|P(f)\big|^2$$
\(P(f)\) is the Fourier transform of one chip pulse. The page computes it exactly for each pulse shape.

MBOC: GPS L1C, Galileo E1 and BeiDou B1C all put 1/11 of their power in BOC(6,1) and 10/11 in BOC(1,1). They do it in different ways: TMBOC (time-multiplexed chips), CBOC (a weighted sum), and QMBOC (in quadrature). The total spectrum is the same, which makes the three signals interoperable.

How big is a chip?

Light travels 293 m during one L1 C/A chip. An L5, E5a or B2a chip is ten times shorter: 29 m. A carrier wavelength is 19 to 25 cm. The school bus (12.2 m) in the top row gives the scale.

  • The code tells you the range to within a fraction of a chip: metres.
  • The carrier tells you range change to within a fraction of a wavelength: millimetres.
  • The code repeats every 300 km (1 ms), 1200 km (4 ms) or 3000 km (10 ms). The secondary code and the message remove that ambiguity.

For GNSS-R from a drone the reflected path is only a few tens to a few hundreds of metres longer than the direct path (Part 10). That is less than one L1 C/A chip, and only a few L5 chips. This is why the 10.23 Mchip/s signals are attractive for HERON.
